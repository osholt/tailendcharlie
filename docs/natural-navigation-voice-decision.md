# Natural and shareable navigation voices - architecture decision

Decision date: 13 August 2026  
Status: accepted for staged implementation  
Tracks: #509, with follow-up work in #512, #513, #514, and #515

## Decision

Tail End Charlie will continue to synthesize each complete spoken instruction
live. Installed operating-system text to speech remains the default and the
only engine allowed to gate a navigation or safety alert. The app will prefer
Daniel for a fresh British-English profile and put installed higher-quality,
non-novelty voices first.

The next supported improvement is better use of platform capabilities:

- iOS may offer installed premium/enhanced voices and, after a separate
  explicit authorization action, the device owner's Personal Voice;
- Android should prefer a high-quality voice whose
  `isNetworkConnectionRequired` value is false; and
- either platform must fall back immediately to an available operating-system
  voice when a saved voice is missing or unavailable offline.

A custom cross-platform voice is a separate downloadable-pack experiment. The
first candidate is a small licensed model running through Sherpa-ONNX, which
documents Flutter, Android arm64, and iOS arm64 support and offline TTS. It may
become a product engine only after it passes #514's physical-device gates. It
will generate and cache whole utterances, not splice recordings or generated
fragments.

Cloud synthesis is not a core navigation path. A network, account, server
capacity, or provider-policy failure must never silence or delay an alert.

## Why complete live utterances are required

The useful speech vocabulary is unbounded. In addition to a finite set of
turns and measurements, the app needs to say text such as:

- “In 400 yards, at the Wickwar Road roundabout, take the third exit.”
- “Continue on the A420 towards Chippenham.”
- “Oliver is off course by half a mile.”
- “Priya has stopped near Siston Common.”

Pre-recording every phrase is therefore impossible. Recording fixed clauses
and inserting separately generated names sounds discontinuous and loses the
intonation that distinguishes the action, distance, and subject. It also makes
pronunciation fixes hard to apply consistently.

The engine receives one normalized sentence. Stable wording still comes from
deterministic app templates; road, place, and rider names are bounded and
sanitized before interpolation. The engine may cache the resulting complete
audio by a hash of normalized text, voice-pack version, locale, rate, and
pronunciation-dictionary version. The cache stays in the app sandbox, uses a
bounded least-recently-used policy, is cleared with the voice pack, and is never
used after its text or voice version changes.

## Platform path

### Apple platforms

`AVSpeechSynthesisVoice.speechVoices()` is the supported catalogue available
to the app. A voice reports its locale and `default`, `enhanced`, or `premium`
quality; recent systems also report `isNoveltyVoice` and `isPersonalVoice`
traits. Tail End Charlie can select an available premium/enhanced voice, but it
must not promise that an exact Siri-branded assistant voice or voice name is
installed on every device.

Personal Voice is owned and generated by the device user. Apple exposes it to
an app only after `AVSpeechSynthesizer.requestPersonalVoiceAuthorization` is
approved. Tail End Charlie will not request that permission at launch, export
the model, upload it, or present it as a voice another rider can install.
#512 owns the native trait bridge, permission UX, and physical evidence.

Apple also supports custom speech-synthesis provider audio-unit extensions.
That is a possible later way to expose an app-owned model to system speech
features, but it does not provide a model, training pipeline, consent, or
real-time performance for free. The first neural experiment will keep one
cross-platform engine behind the app's existing speech interface; an iOS
provider extension is justified only after that engine passes the benchmark.

### Android

Android `Voice` metadata includes quality, latency, features, and whether a
network connection is required. Android's own guidance is to select a voice
whose `isNetworkConnectionRequired()` value is false for embedded synthesis.
Network voices may remain selectable when labelled, but cannot be the product
default and cannot be the only fallback. #513 owns missing-data guidance,
airplane-mode evidence, and engine fallback.

### Existing Flutter boundary

The current `flutter_tts` version already returns Apple quality/gender/
identifier and Android quality/latency/network/features metadata. #508/PR #511
starts retaining the useful quality and network fields, prefers Daniel, and
keeps the novelty-heavy long list behind an explicit action. Native work is
still needed for Apple's voice traits and Personal Voice authorization.

## Custom and shared voice packs

“Shared” means a signed, versioned synthesis model and its public metadata can
be installed by another app user. It does not mean sharing Apple Personal
Voice data, arbitrary recordings, cached phrases, or a model cloned from a
person without recorded permission.

Every future pack must contain a manifest with:

- immutable pack and model version identifiers;
- SHA-256 hashes for every executable/model asset;
- supported locale, sample rate, engine ABI, and minimum app version;
- code and model licence plus redistribution rights;
- whether the voice is wholly synthetic or based on recordings;
- for a recording-derived voice, the speaker's explicit consent scope,
  territory, expiry/revocation path, and approved display name; and
- the publisher signature and revocation identifier.

The first experiment must use a voice whose code, weights, and training/voice
licence permit redistribution and whose identity is not a real colleague or
celebrity. User-created cloning and peer-to-peer pack import are out of scope
until consent verification, abuse reporting, signature trust, and revocation
have a reviewed design. Deleting a pack deletes its generated-audio cache.

#515 owns product distribution and remains blocked unless #514 passes.

## MrDonut Radio evidence

The MrDonut work established two useful facts:

- Kokoro-FastAPI is the smaller Apache-2.0 fallback and can synthesize complete
  dynamic text, but its current deployment is a CPU server container rather
  than a measured phone runtime.
- The more expressive Chatterbox presenter is deliberately pre-rendered on an
  8 GB host, allows a five-minute synthesis timeout, and unloads temporary
  model memory after each link. That makes it evidence for high-quality
  offline programme production, not a viable assumption for a live alert on a
  phone.

Sherpa-ONNX is the useful bridge from that research: it supports offline
Kokoro, Piper/VITS and other TTS families on mobile architectures and has a
Flutter example. Model support is not product evidence, so #514 must measure a
specific pinned engine and model on the oldest supported physical phones.

## Neural voice experiment gates

The spike fails safely; failure leaves installed OS TTS as the product design.
Measure release-mode arm64 builds while the real map, route matching, location,
and alert scheduler are active.

| Gate | Pass condition on the lowest supported iPhone and Android handset |
| --- | --- |
| Download | target at most 150 MB compressed; reject above 250 MB for one English voice |
| Memory | no more than 300 MB additional peak resident memory and no app/OS memory warning |
| First audio | p95 cold whole-utterance start at most 750 ms; cached start at most 100 ms |
| Throughput | real-time factor at most 0.5 for the pronunciation corpus |
| Cancellation | audible output stops within 150 ms when a newer safety alert supersedes it |
| Reliability | 500 consecutive mixed prompts with no crash, hang, truncated action, or missed OS-TTS fallback |
| Offline | cold and warm generation pass in airplane mode after the pack is installed |
| Battery/thermal | a 60-minute simulated ride adds no more than 5 percentage points over OS TTS and reaches no serious thermal state |
| Pronunciation | at least 95% of the UK corpus is understood correctly, with zero wrong action, exit number, distance, or rider identity |

If first audio has not started within 800 ms, the app cancels the neural result
and speaks the same complete utterance through OS TTS. A cached or late result
must never play after that fallback.

The corpus includes difficult UK road/place names, letter-number road names,
abbreviations after normalization, roundabout exits, decimal and singular
measurements, and rider names from several language backgrounds. Results must
be retained with device/build/model identifiers, not summarized as “sounded
good”.

## Alternatives rejected for the core path

- **Pre-generate all navigation audio:** cannot cover arbitrary road, place, or
  rider names.
- **Splice recorded or synthesized slots:** noticeably weaker prosody and
  creates two pronunciation/caching systems for one sentence.
- **Use cloud neural TTS for every prompt:** violates offline-first navigation
  and adds latency, recurring cost, data disclosure, and a new safety-critical
  availability dependency.
- **Ship Chatterbox from the radio stack on phones now:** the existing
  pre-rendered server profile provides no mobile latency, memory, battery, or
  thermal evidence.
- **Allow arbitrary cloned voices immediately:** provenance, consent,
  impersonation, deletion, and revocation are unsolved product and safety
  requirements.
